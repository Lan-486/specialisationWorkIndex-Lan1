Aquarium with Me — Parallel Prototypes

Home page Assignment 1

This page documents six parallel prototypes developed for Aquarium with Me, a browser-based dynamic Soundtrack designed to create a calm and continuously changing underwater sound environment.

Design Area 1: Drag Interaction and Layout

For the first design area, I focused on the layout of the drag-and-drop interaction. In Aquarium with Me, each draggable element represents a different sound layer, while the drop zone represents the aquarium where the sound becomes active. I kept the basic interaction consistent across all three prototypes so that the main difference would come from the layout rather than from a completely different interaction. I wanted to explore whether the position of the controls and the aquarium would change how users understand the relationship between them. I was also interested in whether the user would see the draggable elements as sound controls, objects that belong in the aquarium, or simply buttons that need to be moved. The three layouts test different directions of movement: from left to right, from below towards the centre, and from above downwards.

Prototype A: Left-to-Right Layout

The draggable elements are placed on the left and the drop zone is placed on the right. This layout explores a clear left-to-right relationship between the controls and the aquarium.

Design Area 2: Random Bubble Generation

For the second design area, I focused on the generation and movement of bubbles. The bubbles are not just decorative elements; they provide a visual response to the user's interaction with the audio instrument. I wanted the aquarium to feel active and continuously changing without requiring the user to control many buttons or sliders. I used the space bar as the main input because it is easy to press and allows the user to interact without moving the mouse around the page. I also used Math.random() as my extended technique. It controls different values such as the bubble size, horizontal position, movement direction and animation duration. This means that the result changes each time the user interacts with the prototype. The three prototypes explore different levels of activity. The first focuses on a simple one-to-one response, the second creates a larger group of bubbles in a focused central area, and the third connects the amount of visual activity to how long the user holds the space bar.

Prototype 1: SingleRandom Bubble

In Prototype 1, each press of the space bar creates one bubble. I designed this version as a simple starting point so that one user action creates one clear visual response. The bubble still changes its size, position and animation duration using Math.random(). This keeps the interaction easy to understand while making each bubble slightly different.

Prototype 2: Centred Bubble Movement

In Prototype 2, one press of the space bar creates several bubbles that rise through the central area of the aquarium. I changed the number of bubbles because I wanted to create a stronger visual response than in Prototype 1. Instead of producing only one bubble, the interaction creates a small group that appears over a short period of time. I placed the bubbles around the centre rather than across the entire screen. This keeps the main activity connected to the centre of the aquarium and makes it easier to see the relationship between the bubbles and the interactive space. However, I did not want the bubbles to form one perfectly straight line, because that would make the movement feel too mechanical. For this reason, I added a random horizontal offset to each bubble. The bubbles still move upwards through a similar area, but their positions change slightly each time. This version focuses on creating a more concentrated and visually layered bubble movement.

Prototype 3: Press and Continuous Stream

In Prototype 3, a short press of the space bar creates bubbles, while holding the space bar creates a continuous stream. I designed this version to make the amount of visual activity respond to the length of the user's input. A short interaction creates a small moment of movement, while a longer interaction produces a more active and crowded aquarium. This changes the space bar from a simple trigger into a more expressive control. The user can decide how much activity they want by changing how long they hold the key. I wanted the interaction to feel connected to the user's physical action, so the bubbles continue appearing only while the space bar is being held. The timing between bubbles is also slightly randomised, which prevents the stream from feeling like a fixed mechanical sequence. The purpose of this prototype was to explore whether holding an input could create a stronger sense of control and rhythm within the aquarium.
